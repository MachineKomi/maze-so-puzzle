## Observed performance clarification

- The web app on the Human's laptop has broadly the same successful layout as the
  iPad and plays about as well as the Tauri app. Both are noticeably better than
  the affected iPad, but neither is consistently buttery smooth.
- On the iPad, turning off effects/movement in v0.22.0 **did** make play smoother.
  It still did not become as smooth or responsive as required.
- Product target: the game should remain extremely smooth on ordinary consumer
  devices appropriate to this kind of 2D game, including after later visual polish.
  Very weak outlier hardware may be excluded only through honest measured minimums.

Interpretation: motion/effect work is a demonstrated contributor, not the sole
established cause. Keep web, Tauri and physical iPad evidence separate.

## Three movement pace modes

Add an easy-to-find UI preference which cycles between exactly three movement
paces:

- **Chill:** deliberately slow and calm for a new or less coordinated player;
- **Regular:** the default, likely a little slower than v0.22.0; and
- **Zippy:** faster than v0.22.0 for confident players and rapid testing.

The names and exact cadence values may be tuned with Human/family evidence. The
preference changes presentation and repeat pace only: every input still resolves
one legal tile step, puzzle rules and solver reachability remain identical, and no
speed grants skipped interactions or queued catch-up. It must work across keyboard,
touch, fixed pad and later controllers, persist safely, and be announced accessibly.
Zippy must be included in performance/save-write qualification rather than treated
as free throughput.

## Alex — future optional Player 1

Alex is a desired future boy Player-1 option for the Human's son. Ame remains the
canonical default. Alex has blue eyes and blonde to slightly brown-blonde hair; his
exact age-appropriate design, clothing, relationship, model sheet, voice/copy and
selection presentation require a later Human art/canon gate.

Alex must have equal capabilities, campaign access, rewards, controls and co-op
compatibility. Do not encode gendered difficulty or a weaker ruleset: the separate
Chill pace preference supplies the desired beginner comfort. Plan 08 and Plan 10
must avoid hard-coding seams that would make the later optional Player-1 slot
needlessly expensive; Plan 10A implements Alex before Plan 11 may depict him in
final key art.

## Tessera Dolphin field-alpha defect

The Human observed that Tessera Dolphin's tail fin and one flipper render partly
transparent in gameplay. Repository inspection confirms the current 256px field
derivative contains missing coral regions. The approved original identity is good,
and the later 512px presentation rendition already demonstrates a bounded alpha
recovery using the original RGB plus a guarded corrected mask.

Repair requires a new versioned field derivative and catalogue pointer, provenance,
byte allocation, field-size light/dark proof and in-game cage/follower/victory
checks. It is an extraction defect, not authorization to redraw Tessera.

## Stationary cage-rescue interaction

Bumping an unresolved cage should rescue the friend **without moving Player 1 into
the cage tile**. The player stays visibly and logically on the adjacent origin tile
for the complete cage opening/friend release. Once the cage has resolved, a later
input—or a still-genuinely-held eligible direction after presentation—may enter the
cleared tile. Turning around must remain possible immediately.

This is the common contact-interaction rule for doors, combat and cages. Ordinary
walk-over consumables and pickups remain pass-through: movement into their tile and
collection may occur in the same legal step. Portals and traversal mechanics retain
their separately specified movement semantics.

Update the engine, solver/reachability, hints/metrics, fingerprints/revisions,
routes, followers, save reconstruction and presentation-continuation fixtures as
one reviewed gameplay change. Rescue state/rewards still commit exactly once.
